A Matter of Flow

Blood flow is invisible, but it plays an important role in what happens inside the aorta. This application is a science-communication project about the aorta and the diseases that can change it. To make these complex processes accessible beyond a medical context, it uses narrative visualization to translate medical imaging, computational models, simulated blood flow, and clinical knowledge into an interactive scrollytelling experience that can be explored at your own pace. This makes abstract processes such as changes in blood flow easier to understand, while making the information more engaging and personally relatable.

The underlying vascular models and datasets come from the Vascular Model Repository, an open-source database of computational models of normal and diseased cardiovascular anatomy. The repository is intended for research; this project creates a visual and narrative entry point for understanding what those models can show. Above all, we hope you will come away valuing transparent explanations as a basis for trust in medical visualizations: knowing what the images and simulations are based on, and what they show. This aim is informed by Lee-Robbins and Adar’s framework for affective learning objectives.

Two Stories

The two stories display two different diseases that affect the aorta and come from Marfan syndrome, an inherited condition that affects connective tissue. In some people, it can make the aortic wall more likely to widen, particularly near the heart; changes vary from person to person.
