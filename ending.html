TWO OUTLOOKS

What the flow made visible

Alex

Aortic aneurysm

In the simulation Blood speeds up in Alex's widened aortic arch and can swirl into a vortex instead of moving in smooth layers.

Alex's outlook Alex leaves the scan with a plan rather than a final answer. His future depends on regular imaging, attention to warning signs, and decisions made early enough to avoid an emergency. The flow view cannot predict what will happen to him, but it makes the need for careful follow-up easier to understand.

Miriam

Aortic dissection

In the simulation Blood in Miriam's dissected aorta no longer follows one route: it moves through both the true and the false lumen.

Miriam's outlook Miriam's future cannot be decided from one scan or one simulation. What remains after the emergency is a long relationship with follow-up care. The aorta is a living vessel, its shape can change, and blood flow can keep influencing the wall long after the first tear.

Neither simulation predicts how Alex's or Miriam's story will continue. What both show is the same link: when the shape of the aorta changes, the flow inside it changes too, and the wall is exposed to that flow with every heartbeat. Making this invisible flow visible helps explain why both of them need long-term follow-up.
